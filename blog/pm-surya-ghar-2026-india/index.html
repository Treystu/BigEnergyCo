PM Surya Ghar 2026: What the Rs 78,000 Subsidy Covers

Last reviewed: November 13, 2026 · Published: November 13, 2026 · Lucas Ballek

Two numbers get quoted at every Indian rooftop-solar enquiry: a Rs 78,000 subsidy and 300 units free a month. Both come from the same scheme, and both are narrower than they sound. This guide takes the terms from the scheme's own guidelines — the central financial assistance table, the benchmark costs, the conditions attached to the money — and then works one household in rupees.

It is worth being exact, because the money is paid against a benchmark and not against the price you were quoted. The gap between those two is your bill, and it is the number a sales conversation is least likely to name.

What the scheme actually pays

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana was approved on 29 February 2024 with an outlay of Rs 75,021 crore and an implementation period to 31 March 2027. The central financial assistance is a percentage of a fixed benchmark cost, and at the benchmark rates in force since 13 February 2024 that resolves to the familiar figures:

Central financial assistance for residential rooftop solar
System size (DC) Benchmark cost Central subsidy
1 kWp Rs 50,000 Rs 30,000
2 kWp Rs 1,00,000 Rs 60,000
2.5 kWp Rs 1,22,500 Rs 69,000
3 kWp Rs 1,45,000 Rs 78,000
5 kWp or more — Rs 78,000 (capped)

The mechanism behind the table: 60% of the benchmark cost for the first 2 kWp, 40% of the benchmark for the third kWp, and no additional central assistance beyond 3 kWp. The benchmark is Rs 50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 45,000 for the third. The MNRE guidelines for central financial assistance to residential consumers carry the table, the worked examples (1.5 kW, 2.5 kW, 6 kW) and the benchmark-cost clause; the National Portal publishes the same rates for consumers.

Three details sit inside that paragraph and change real money:

  • Special category states get 10% more. Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, the North-Eastern states including Sikkim, and the union territories of Andaman and Nicobar and Lakshadweep are on Rs 33,000 and Rs 19,800 per kW, so a 3 kWp system there carries Rs 85,800 of central assistance.
  • The subsidy follows DC capacity, not the inverter. The guidelines are explicit that assistance is paid on the rated DC capacity of the modules, so pairing a large array with a smaller inverter does not shrink the claim, and a large inverter does not enlarge it.
  • Group housing is a separate line. Societies and resident welfare associations get Rs 18,000 per kW for common facilities, up to 500 kW, capped at 3 kW per house and inclusive of what individual residents install.

Why 3 kW is the ceiling, and what that does to a 5 kW plan

The cap is the most consequential number in the scheme and the least discussed. A household that needs 5 kWp receives the same Rs 78,000 as one that installs 3 kWp, so the fourth and fifth kilowatts are paid for entirely by the owner.

Same subsidy, very different cost per subsidised kW
System Subsidy Subsidy per kWp
2 kWp Rs 60,000 Rs 30,000
3 kWp Rs 78,000 Rs 26,000
5 kWp Rs 78,000 Rs 15,600
10 kWp Rs 78,000 Rs 7,800

This does not mean 3 kW is always right. It means the subsidy stops being the reason to add capacity at 3 kWp, and the case for a larger system has to stand on its own — on the units it generates and the tariff they displace. A household with two air conditioners and a 600-unit bill should still size to its load; it should simply not expect the scheme to fund the part above 3 kW.

The conditions attached to the money

These are from the guidelines rather than from vendor leaflets, and each one has refused a claim somewhere:

  • Apply through the National Portal only. The application, the vendor selection and the claim all run through pmsuryaghar.gov.in, and the vendor must be registered on it.
  • Domestically manufactured cells and modules. Modules built from DCR cells are an essential condition of eligibility. A cheaper import that fails this test is not a subsidised system at all.
  • Grid-connected, on a roof, terrace, balcony or elevated structure. Off-grid installations are explicitly not eligible. Behind-the-meter systems with batteries are eligible subject to the electricity regulator's approval.
  • One claim per installation, once. A system that is relocated does not qualify again, and a household that already took central assistance can claim only for the balance up to 3 kWp overall.
  • A DISCOM inspection before the money moves. The distribution company inspects the system physically, signs the metering agreement and approves the claim; the guidelines commit to processing the CFA within 15 days of that approval.
  • A ceiling on the scheme itself. Applications on the portal are limited to one crore, and the implementation period runs to 31 March 2027. Neither is a reason to rush a bad installation, but both are reasons to check the portal's current state before assuming a subsidy will be waiting.

The 300 units line, stated honestly

The scheme's objective is to help provide free or low-cost electricity to one crore households up to 300 units a month by installing rooftop solar. That is a generation target, not a payment: the units are "free" because you made them, and the bill you still receive contains the fixed charges your connection carries.

The arithmetic behind 300 units is straightforward. A 3 kWp system in most of India generates roughly 330 to 400 units a month across the year, so a 3 kWp installation is the size at which a modest household can plausibly offset 300 units. Below that you will still buy units; above it you will export, and what an exported unit is worth depends on your state.

A 3 kWp household, worked in rupees

The assumptions are stated because the answer moves with them: 3 kWp at benchmark, 360 units generated a month, 300 units consumed, and an energy charge of Rs 8 a unit. Your state's slab structure will differ — this is arithmetic, not a tariff quote.

Benchmark cost, subsidy and monthly saving
Line Rupees
Installed cost at benchmark, 3 kWp 1,45,000
Central subsidy -78,000
Your cost at benchmark 67,000
Energy charge avoided, 300 units at Rs 8 2,400 a month
Simple recovery of your 67,000 about 28 months

Two honest adjustments. At a Rs 6 energy charge the same 300 units are worth Rs 1,800 a month and recovery stretches to about 37 months. And if the quote is above benchmark — say Rs 1,80,000 for the same 3 kWp — the subsidy is still Rs 78,000, so your cost is Rs 1,02,000 and the recovery stretches again. The scheme does not close the gap between a benchmark and a quote.

The 60 units a month you generate beyond your own use are exported. What they earn is set by your state's regulation, not by the central scheme, which is the next section.

Metering is a state question, not a scheme question

The guidelines accept every metering arrangement a state regulator approves: net metering, net billing, gross metering, virtual net metering and group net metering. That sentence does a lot of work, because the arrangements are not equivalent.

  • Net metering offsets your import with your export at the retail energy charge, usually within a billing period, with surplus treated per the state's rules.
  • Net billing buys your export at a separate, typically lower, rate and bills your import in full. Two identical roofs in two states can therefore have different paybacks.
  • Capacity conditions apply either way. Several states restrict net metering above a threshold, and above 10 kW the system size is commonly tied to your sanctioned load.

Before you size anything, read your own state electricity regulatory commission's current order and your DISCOM's net-metering procedure. The central subsidy is uniform; the value of a generated unit is not.

The loan terms, and what they are worth

The scheme provides collateral-free loans for residential systems up to 3 kW at a rate tied to the repo rate plus 50 basis points — around 7% at current settings — through participating banks. The useful part is not the headline rate but the interaction with the subsidy: where the CFA exceeds the outstanding loan, it is credited to the loan account up to the amount outstanding and the remainder goes to your bank account.

Compare the loan rate against what the system actually saves. At Rs 2,400 a month of avoided energy on Rs 67,000 of net cost, borrowing at 7% is cheaper than the saving it produces, which is the only test that matters. If your state's energy charge is low enough that the saving is smaller than the interest, the loan is doing the opposite of its purpose.

What to ask a vendor, in order

  • Is the quote per kWp at or above benchmark? Ask for the number split into modules, inverter, structure, wiring, meter and installation. The subsidy is fixed, so this is the only part you can negotiate.
  • Are the modules DCR? Ask for the certificate, not the brochure claim. Without it the claim fails and the price you agreed was for an unsubsidised system.
  • Who signs the metering agreement, and how long does your DISCOM take? Ask for their last three installations in your area and the date each net meter was fitted.
  • What does the five-year maintenance cover? Registered vendors are required to provide free repair and maintenance for five years under the scheme's comprehensive maintenance contract. Get the scope in writing.
  • What happens if the DISCOM rejects the claim? The subsidy is paid after inspection, so the answer should be a named remedy, not a reassurance.

Frequently asked

How much subsidy does PM Surya Ghar pay in 2026?

Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 18,000 for the third kW, so Rs 60,000 for a 2 kW system and Rs 78,000 for a 3 kW system or anything larger. There is no central subsidy beyond 3 kW. Special category states and union territories get 10% more.

Does a system larger than 3 kW get a larger subsidy?

No. The central financial assistance stops at 3 kW of DC capacity, so a 5 kW system receives the same Rs 78,000 as a 3 kW system, and every extra kWp is paid for entirely by you. State top-ups, where they exist, follow their own rules.

Is the 300 units of free electricity a cash payment?

No. The scheme's objective is to provide free or low-cost electricity up to 300 units a month by having the household generate it. You still pay fixed charges, and any units you draw beyond your generation are billed at your state's tariff.

Can I claim the subsidy on a system I installed myself?

The guidelines require applying through the National Portal and using a vendor registered on it, with domestically manufactured DCR modules, a DISCOM inspection and a signed metering agreement. A self-installed system that bypassed those steps does not qualify, and a rooftop system is eligible for the central subsidy only once.
