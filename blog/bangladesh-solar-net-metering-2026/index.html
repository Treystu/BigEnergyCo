Bangladesh Net Metering 2026: BERC Slabs and Taka Math

Last reviewed: November 13, 2026 · Published: November 13, 2026 · Lucas Ballek

Bangladesh changed both halves of the rooftop-solar arithmetic in the space of a year. In June 2026 the regulator reset the residential tariff slabs. In September 2026 the Power Division put a price on exported solar. This guide reads both at the source, then does the only thing that settles a rooftop decision: the sum, in taka, for one household.

If you have seen a payback figure for Dhaka with no slab table behind it, that figure is a guess. The bill you avoid is built from slabs, a demand charge and VAT, and the units you export are paid at a different rate with a deduction attached.

What a residential bill is made of in 2026

BERC sets one national retail schedule and the six distribution companies apply it. The June 2026 order raised the weighted average retail tariff to Tk 10.40 a unit, after a 4 June amendment restored the lifeline and first-slab rates the 3 June order had raised.

Two mechanics matter more than the numbers. Slabs are incremental: the units fill the cheap steps first and only the surplus reaches the expensive one. And the slab rates are the energy charge only — a demand charge per kW of sanctioned load and 5% VAT are added on top. The June 2026 rates and the incremental method are set out in The Business Standard's September 2026 walk-through of DESCO and DPDC billing, and the 4 June 2026 amendment that kept the first two slabs unchanged is reported by The Daily Star. Prepaid and postpaid meters use the same rates.

The net-metering rules that decide what your panels are worth

The Net Energy Metering portal and the SREDA policy library carry the Net Metering Guideline 2025. Four clauses do the work:

  • Capacity. The aggregate AC output of your inverters may be up to 100% of your sanctioned load, up from 70% in the 2018 guideline. Consumers on medium voltage and above are additionally capped at 80% of the transformer's rated capacity.
  • Who can apply. Single-phase consumers and prepaid or smart-meter customers are now eligible; under the 2018 rules only three-phase consumers could participate.
  • The 10% deduction. The utility takes 10% of net exported electricity in a billing period as a distribution system maintenance charge and credits the remaining 90%.
  • Settlement. Credits are reconciled quarterly, after March, June, September and December. Credit left at settlement is paid to your bank or mobile-banking account, and the balance resets.

Then there is the export price. In September 2026 the Power Division announced Tk 10.50 per kWh for surplus power from eligible net-metered rooftop systems installed by 28 February 2027, running for three years to 28 February 2030 and paid quarterly to the consumer's bank account. The same package set a benchmark generation cost of Tk 8 per kWh for rooftop systems with battery storage. The terms, the deadline and the add-on margin and premium are reported by pv magazine; the guideline itself is the document to read before you sign, and your utility's implementation is what your bill will actually show.

A 400-unit household, worked in taka

Take a Dhaka household using 400 units a month on a 1 kW sanctioned load. Energy charge first, because that is the part solar can touch:

Add the fixed demand charge for 1 kW and 5% VAT on the total and the bill lands near Tk 3,540 a month. That is the number a solar system is competing with, and the same method reproduces the published worked example for a 100-unit household, which is a useful check that the slab arithmetic above is right.

With a 3 kW rooftop system

A 3 kW array in Bangladesh generates roughly 350 to 420 units a month depending on the site, so take 360. Assume 80% is used in the house and 20% is exported. Those are stated assumptions, not a promise, and you should replace them with your own meter readings.

The bill falls by about Tk 2,751 for 288 units you no longer buy — an average of Tk 9.55 per self-consumed unit, not the Tk 9.62 top-slab rate, because cutting consumption walks you back down through cheaper slabs. The 72 exported units earn roughly Tk 680, or Tk 9.45 each after the 10% deduction.

About Tk 41,000 a year against SREDA's own calculator default of Tk 70,000 per kWp installed, which prices a 3 kWp system at roughly Tk 2.1 lakh. That is a simple payback near five years, before tariff changes, inverter replacement or a battery. It is a calculation with named assumptions, not a quote — get the quote in writing.

Why self-consumption still beats export here

Bangladesh is unusual: at Tk 10.50 the export rate sits close to the Tk 10.40 weighted-average retail rate, so exporting is not the near-worthless gesture it is on some grids. But close is not equal, and the comparison is not against the average. It is against the slab the next unit would have cost you.

  • Above 400 units a self-consumed unit avoids Tk 15.01, against Tk 9.45 net for an exported one. The gap is wider than the whole export rate.
  • Above 600 units it avoids Tk 17.35. A household in that band should treat export as the residual, not the plan.
  • Below 200 units the arithmetic reverses. The slabs you displace are Tk 5.26 and Tk 8.50, so exported units can be worth more than the ones you use, and a grid-tied system with no battery is the sensible shape.

Which is to say the right system size depends on which slab you are standing on. Sizing to "cover the bill" usually oversizes a small consumer and undersizes a large one.

Eligibility, roof area, and the application path

The capacity rule is written against your connection, so read your bill first: the sanctioned load, in kW, is the ceiling for your inverters' combined AC output. A 3 kW array on a 2 kW sanctioned load does not qualify as submitted.

Roof space is the other constraint, and SREDA's net-metering calculator uses 9 square metres per kWp as its default. A 3 kWp array therefore needs about 27 square metres of shadow-free roof, which in practice means more than 27 square metres of roof.

The application itself is online at the NEM portal and runs in six steps: application, inspection, approval, installation, evaluation, agreement. The portal states that inspection to approval takes at most ten working days. Two things to check on your own connection before you apply: whether your meter is prepaid or smart (now eligible, but confirm your utility has wired it) and, above single phase, whether your transformer has headroom for 80% of its rating.

What to verify before you sign

  • Inverter AC output, not module kWp. The capacity test is applied to the inverters. A design that passes on DC capacity can fail on AC output.
  • The 28 February 2027 date. The Tk 10.50 rate is tied to systems installed by that date. If your installation slips, the export revenue you modelled may not exist.
  • Quarterly, not monthly. Credits settle after March, June, September and December. A system sized to export heavily is lending the utility three months of energy before it is paid.
  • The 10% deduction and the fixed charges. Neither is avoidable. They belong in your model, because a payback that ignores them is short by roughly a tenth of every exported unit plus the demand charge for the whole system life.

Frequently asked

How much surplus solar can I export in Bangladesh?

Under the Net Metering Guideline 2025 the aggregate AC output of your inverters may be up to 100% of your sanctioned load, up from 70% in the 2018 guideline. Consumers on medium and higher voltage are also limited to 80% of the transformer's rated capacity.

What is the Tk 10.50 export tariff?

In September 2026 the Power Division announced Tk 10.50 per kWh for surplus power from eligible net-metered rooftop systems installed by 28 February 2027. It runs for three years to 28 February 2030 and is paid quarterly to the consumer's bank account. Confirm your own eligibility with your distribution utility before you buy.

Does a 10% charge come off exported units?

Yes. The 2025 guideline takes 10% of net exported electricity in a billing period as a distribution system maintenance charge and credits the remaining 90%. A net export of 72 units becomes 64.8 credit units.

Is solar worth more used at home than exported?

For a household in the upper slabs, yes. A self-consumed unit avoids the slab rate you would otherwise pay, Tk 15.01 or Tk 17.35 above 400 and 600 units. An exported unit is worth about Tk 9.45 after the 10% charge at the 2026 incentive rate. Use the units yourself where you can.
