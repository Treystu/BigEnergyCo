Net Plus, Sri Lanka 2026: what your roof now earns

Last checked: 14 November 2026 · Published: 14 November 2026 · Lucas Ballek

සිංහල සාරාංශය: 2026 මාර්තු 5 න් පසු නව සූර්ය සම්බන්ධතා සඳහා නෙට් මීටරින්ග් වසා ඇත; දැන් සියල්ල "Net Plus" යටතේ විකුණනු ලැබේ — එක් යුනිටයක් සඳහා රු. 23.11 (10 kW දක්වා). නමුත් ඔබ පරිභෝජනය කරන සෑම යුනිටයක්ම ඔබ ගෙවන්නේ සාමාන්‍ය ගාස්තුවට — ඒකක 180 ට වැඩි නම් රු. 100 දක්වා. එබැවින් එකම වහල, එකම පද්ධතිය, දැන් වැඩි මිලක්.

On 5 March 2026 Sri Lanka's National Electricity Policy took the country's rooftop market off net metering: every new on-grid agreement, and every extension of an old one, must run on a Net Plus basis. A Ministry of Energy directive dated 11 September 2026 told distribution licensees to stop accepting Net Metering and Net Accounting applications altogether, to cap new Net Plus agreements at 12 years, and to require smart meters. What did not change is the price: PUCSL's feed-in tariff decision of 21 August 2026, effective 25 August, still pays.

A 2.5% Social Security Contribution Levy applies on top of the monthly bill. Domestic rates are block-based: your total consumption picks the band, and the band prices the units. The full block table runs 5.00 / 9.00 / 20.00 / 28.00 / 44.00 / 32.50 / 100.00 from the PUCSL Annex 2 of the May 2026 decision.

Why the same roof is now worth less

Net metering cancelled units against each other: a unit you generated erased a unit you would have bought, at whatever the marginal block cost. Net Plus does not cancel anything. Every generated unit is sold at the feed-in tariff, and every unit you consume is bought at your block rate — separately, on two meters. When your marginal block is LKR 100 and the feed-in tariff is LKR 23.11, the two meters are not a formality; they are a 4.3× spread, and it runs against the household.

That is the whole story, and it is not the story most Sri Lankan solar pages tell. The system did not get worse; the settlement changed. Under Net Plus the value of a rooftop comes from the rate you avoid versus the rate you are paid, and for a household in the top band the gap is enormous.

The arithmetic above is deliberately conservative in one direction and generous in another: it ignores the 60 banked units net metering used to carry forward (which made it better still), and it ignores the 2.5% SSCL on both bills (which scales both columns). The comparison it shows is the direction, not the cent.

What actually works under Net Plus

  • Cut consumption before sizing generation. Under Net Plus the block you land in sets your import price; dropping from the above-180 band to 121–180 changes the rate from 100 to 44 on the balance.
  • Look hard at the battery tariff. The same decision creates a separate feed-in tariff for new rooftop PV with storage: LKR 45.53/kWh at the priority feed-in time for the first 15 years. That is roughly double the solar-only rate, and it is the only route the schedule offers back toward the old value.
  • Check the 12-year term. Net Plus agreements are capped at 12 years, so any payback beyond that horizon is outside the contract's life.
  • Model it, do not quote it. The calculator takes your monthly units and your tariff and shows what a system does to the bill you actually pay.

Frequently asked

Can I still get net metering if I applied before March?

Existing agreements keep their terms until they expire; extensions move to Net Plus. The 11 September directive closed new applications and extensions under the old schemes.

Do I still pay for the units my panels make?

You pay for every unit you consume, at your block rate, and you are paid for every unit you generate, at the feed-in tariff. Under Net Plus the two are measured separately.

Is the feed-in tariff fixed for the whole agreement?

The approved rates are effective from 25 August 2026 until the next tariff revision. The 12-year agreement term and the revision cycle are separate things, so read your agreement for which rate it locks.

Frequently asked

Is the 8.83% increase confirmed?

The 8.83% overall path was set in NERSA's MYPD6 redetermination of 7 February 2026. The 26 November 2026 decision is on the tariff structure and price schedule that recovers it, which is where the fixed-versus-energy split is set. Check NERSA's published decision after that date rather than relying on a headline.

Will solar reduce my fixed monthly charge?

No. A fixed service or network charge is billed for the connection. Solar displaces the per-kWh energy line, and a battery shifts when you buy from the grid.

Does a bigger system always save more?

Not in rand. Once your daytime load is covered, extra generation is exported at the tariff's credit rather than avoiding the full energy rate, so the marginal saving per extra kW falls. Size against the energy you displace.

What changes for municipal customers?

Municipal tariffs normally follow on 1 July rather than 1 April, and municipalities set their own charge structures within NERSA's approval. Read your own municipality's schedule; the Eskom split does not transfer automatically.
